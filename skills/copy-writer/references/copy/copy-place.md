# Copy for places people visit

Cafés, restaurants, hotels, gyms, shops, salons, galleries. Most visitors are on a phone, already interested, and want a few plain facts so they can go.

## Who reads it

Someone deciding whether to come, today or soon. They arrive with a short list of questions:

1. Where are you?
2. Are you open?
3. What do you have, and what does it cost?
4. Can I book?
5. What is it like?

Answer the first four before the fifth.

## Fact sheet: ask for these

- Name, street, district, how to get there, parking.
- Opening hours, including exceptions and closed days.
- Menu or services with current prices.
- How to book or order. Phone number if it may be shown.
- Accessibility: steps, lift, dog policy, children.
- Real details: who runs it, what is made on site, since when.
- Photographs of the actual place and food.

## Sections

| Section | How to write it |
|---|---|
| Hero | What and where, in one line: "Ramen bar in Kreuzberg". Sub-line: today's hours or the key fact |
| Hours and location | High up the page and in the footer on every page. Plain text, never only in an image |
| Menu or services | Real text in HTML, with prices. A PDF is hard to read on a phone and hard for search engines |
| Book or order | One clear action, the same wording everywhere |
| The place | A few sentences and many photographs |
| Who runs it | Real people and real story, if there is one. Otherwise leave it out |
| Getting here | Street, transit, parking, accessible entrance |
| Reviews | Real, with source |

Accuracy is the whole game. Wrong hours lose trust faster than a plain design does. Mark anything you assumed as `(sample)` in the fact sheet, and list it for the client to confirm.

## Writing

- **Names and streets, not atmosphere.** "Opposite the tram depot" is better than "in the heart of the city".
- **Say what is really there:** "Twelve tables and a counter." "Bread baked here from 5 a.m."
- **Prices are copy.** "Double room €95, parking included" does more than a mood line.
- **Say what's awkward:** "Cash only." "Stairs to the upper dining room." "No bookings at weekends."
- **Menu descriptions are short:** ingredients and how it's cooked. "Leeks, brown butter, hazelnuts."
- **Avoid:** nestled, vibrant, cozy, hidden gem, culinary journey, passion for food, "something for everyone".

## Hours

- Write them consistently: "Tuesday to Sunday, 7:30–16:00". Say "Closed Monday".
- Keep them in one place in the code, so that they can't disagree.
- Show holiday changes clearly.
- Only build an "open now" indicator from hours the client has confirmed.

## Tone

From the place itself. A neighbourhood café is relaxed and plain. A fine-dining room says little and precisely. A gym is direct. Practical information stays plain whatever the voice.

## Microcopy

- Booking button: the thing. "Book a table", "Reserve a room". If there is no booking: "Get directions".
- Form: "Date", "Time", "Guests". Say what happens next: "We'll confirm by email within a few hours", if true.

## Checklist

- [ ] Address and hours are readable within one screen on a phone, as text.
- [ ] The menu is in the page, with prices.
- [ ] Hours appear once in the code and agree everywhere.
- [ ] Every photo is of this place, or is listed as a stand-in.
- [ ] A visitor could arrive without calling.
