# Copy for a software or tech product site

The job is to get the right people to try it or talk to sales, and to send the wrong people away quickly.

## Who reads it

Someone comparing this with what they use now, often a spreadsheet or a known rival. Often there are two readers: the person who will use it and the person who will pay for it.

Their questions, in order:

1. What is this?
2. Is it for someone like me?
3. How is it different from what I use?
4. How does it work?
5. Who else uses it?
6. What does it cost?
7. What happens if I want to leave, or if it breaks?

## Fact sheet: ask for these

- The category, in the words a buyer would search.
- The best-fit customer, and who should not buy.
- What they use today, and why they switch. Exact customer words if possible.
- What it does that the alternative does not, with proof.
- Real numbers: customers, volume, uptime, speed, each with a date or source.
- Customers who may be named. Quotes, word for word.
- Pricing, trial terms, card required or not.
- Integrations by name. Security and data facts.
- Real screens to show.

## Sections

Common order, seen on real product sites and in practitioner guides. No controlled study proves any order is best past the hero, so follow the argument.

| Section | Reader's question | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Am I in the right place? | Category and buyer, or a specific claim. Sub-line: how it works |
| Product shown | What does it look like? | Real screen with real-looking data. Few words |
| Customer logos | Can I trust this? | Only real customers who may be shown |
| Problem | Do you understand me? | The situation in the customer's words. Short |
| How it works | What do I actually do? | Verb-led steps |
| Features | Will it do what I need? | Outcome first, then the feature as proof. As many as there are |
| Integrations | Does it fit my setup? | Names, with real logos |
| Proof | Who else? | Named quote or case with a number |
| Pricing | What will I pay? | See below |
| FAQ | What stops me? | Real objections: setup, migration, security, cancelling |
| Final call to action | What now? | Restate the promise with one fact, same button wording |

## Headlines

Real examples of the plain approach: Linear's "The product development system for teams and agents", Stripe's "Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue." Both name the category and the buyer or benefit. Follow that pattern, not those words.

- "Inventory counts for independent pharmacies"
- "Error tracking for Rails apps"
- "Payroll that closes in one afternoon"

**Sub-line:** the mechanism and the main gain, in 15 to 30 words. One mechanism or one fact.

Avoid a fill-in-the-blanks headline, where only the nouns would change for a competitor. If AI is the point, say what it does: "Reads each delivery note and updates the count."

## Features

- **Lead with what the user gets, then how.** "Know which stock expires first. Every delivery is logged with its batch date."
- **Show real values:** times, sizes, counts. "Customisable" tells the reader nothing, so say what can be changed.
- **Titles are a verb and a thing:** "Flags expired stock".
- **Count them honestly.** Five real features are five cards. Do not pad to a neat grid or cut to three.
- **Name the product the same way every time.**

## Pricing

The tone goes flat here: the reader is deciding about money.

- Plan names mean something. Say who each one is for.
- List what changes between plans, in the same order.
- Say the real unit: per seat, per month, billed annually.
- State the terms beside the button. "Free for a month. Cancel from settings."
- Only mark a plan as recommended if it really is.
- No price? Say how to get one and how long it takes.

## FAQ

Real questions people ask before buying. Answer the first word with yes or no when you can. Admit limits: "No. We don't support on-premise installs yet."

## Tone

Specific, confident and calm. Technical buyers trust exact terms and distrust adjectives. Developer tools can be more technical and drier; a consumer app can be warmer.

## Microcopy

- Sign-up button: the real next step. "Start free trial", "Create account".
- "No credit card required" only when true.
- Security and compliance: name the standard you actually hold.

## Checklist

- [ ] Swap in a rival's name: the headline is no longer true.
- [ ] Every number has a source in the fact sheet.
- [ ] Every logo and quote is real and allowed.
- [ ] A reader can find the price, or how to get it.
- [ ] Features describe outcomes, each with a concrete detail.
