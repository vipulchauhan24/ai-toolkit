# Copy for shops and product pages

The reader can't touch the thing. Words and pictures have to answer everything a person would find out by holding it, and everything that makes them hesitate at the till.

## Who reads it

Someone comparing a few options, often on a phone, looking for a reason to buy or a reason not to.

Their questions:

1. What is it, exactly?
2. Will it work for me: size, fit, compatibility?
3. How much is it, all in?
4. When will it arrive?
5. What if I don't like it?
6. What do people who bought it say?

## Fact sheet: ask for these

- Name, price, variants, stock.
- Materials, dimensions, weight, what's in the box.
- How and where it is made, if true and interesting.
- Who it suits, and who it doesn't.
- Delivery costs and times by region. Returns terms.
- Real reviews with the rating count. Care instructions, certifications.
- A photo for every claim.

## Page parts

| Part | How to write it |
|---|---|
| Product name | What it is, in plain words. "Beech coat rack, five hooks", not only a brand word |
| Price | Visible beside the name. Say if tax or delivery is extra |
| Short description | Two or three sentences: what it is, who it's for, the one thing that sets it apart |
| Variant labels | Real names and what they mean. "Natural oak", with the swatch changing the photo |
| Add to cart | The action. "Add to cart", or "Pre-order, ships in June" if it differs |
| Delivery and returns | Right by the button, specific. "Arrives in 3–5 days. Free returns for 30 days." |
| Details | Scannable specs in a table: dimensions, materials, weight |
| Long description | For what specs can't say: how it feels, how it's used |
| Reviews | Real, unedited, with the count. Show the critical ones too |
| FAQ | Size, care, compatibility |

## Writing about the thing

- **Physical and measurable words:** "58 mm", "wood-fired", "machine washable". A comfortable chair is a claim; a seat 45 cm high with a 5 cm foam pad is a fact, and readers can judge it.
- **Picture it in use:** "Fits under a standard desk." "Holds a 16-inch laptop with the charger."
- **Answer the doubt:** "Runs small. Most people take a size up."
- **Back claims with photos.** If the copy says it folds flat, show it folded.
- **Say each thing in one place.** The description should not repeat the specs in different words.

## Collection and shop pages

- Category names are the words people search for.
- Each product card: name, price, one distinguishing detail if needed.
- Filters use real attributes, and say when no result matches.

## Trust and cost

Surprise costs lose the sale. State delivery, tax and returns before checkout. Don't manufacture urgency: no fake countdowns or "only 3 left" unless it is true.

Sustainability and origin claims need specifics: name the certification, the factory, the percentage.

## Checkout microcopy

- Tone goes flat. No jokes near a payment.
- Labels are standard: "Card number", "Name on card".
- Say what happens next: "You'll get a confirmation email and tracking when it ships."
- Errors say how to fix it: "This card has expired. Try another."

## Tone

Set by the price and the buyer. A hardware shop is plain; a fashion label can say less and let photographs work. Whatever the voice, specs and costs are stated plainly.

## Checklist

- [ ] Someone could buy this without emailing a question.
- [ ] Price, delivery time and returns are near the button.
- [ ] Each adjective has a measurement or a photo behind it.
- [ ] Reviews and scarcity claims are real.
