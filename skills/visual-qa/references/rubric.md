# Grading rubric

Grade each screenshot on what you can see. Seven areas get a letter and a reason. The eighth, motion, is worked out from measurements, because still images cannot show it.

## What each letter means

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Looks deliberately designed. A good designer would change nothing important |
| B | Solid and professional, with small flaws you have to look for |
| C | Works, but looks generic, uneven or unfinished in places. Average AI or template output belongs here |
| D | Visibly wrong: a visitor would notice the problem without looking for it |
| F | Broken or unusable at this size |

**Be strict.** A clean page that follows every rule but has no point of view is a C, not a B. Reserve A for pages you would show as a good example. Most first builds should land between C and B. Do not raise a grade because the work was hard or the rest of the page is good.

## The areas

| Area | A looks like | C looks like | D or F looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| `layout` | One clear focal point per screen, a visible structure, equal margins, sections that differ for a reason | Centred column of similar blocks, stock card rows, correct but anonymous | Lopsided page, colliding blocks, no hierarchy, content pushed to one side |
| `spacing` | A steady rhythm: related things close, sections clearly apart, every empty area closed by something | Mostly even but monotonous, or a few gaps too tight or too loose | Big holes of plain background, cramped text, inconsistent gaps |
| `typography` | Few sizes with real contrast between them, a type pairing with character, comfortable line length | Default font, sizes too close together, or dense small text | Unreadably small, broken line breaks, headlines that overflow |
| `color` | A restrained palette with a clear accent used for meaning, comfortable contrast, designed dark theme | Safe grey plus one accent, or a dark theme that is just inverted | Clashing colours, hard-to-read text, an accent on everything |
| `imagery` | Real images, screenshots or logos that add information, consistent icons, sharp and well cropped | Few visuals, stock-looking images, icons only for decoration | Broken, stretched or blurry images, mixed icon styles, empty frames. Use `N/A` only if the page has no reason to have images |
| `responsive` | Recomposed for this width: sensible stacking, re-cut images, right-sized text | Just the desktop page squeezed, but nothing breaks | Sideways scroll, text overflow, overlaps, buttons too small |
| `accessibility` | Clear headings and labels, visible states, readable text, nothing conveyed by colour alone | Mostly fine, with small gaps | Missing labels, unreadable states, focus or text problems |

## How to write a reason

One line that points at something visible and specific.

- Good: "Hero headline and proof panel give a clear focal point, but the three experience cards below are the same shape."
- Good: "About 900px of empty background under Oodles before Skills."
- Bad: "Good layout." "Could be improved." "Looks clean and modern."

Grade each screenshot on its own. Do not copy one reason across viewports unless the same thing is really true of both, and name the width when a problem exists only there.

## Summary and fixes

- `summary`: two or three sentences a person can act on. Say what is working and what most holds the site back.
- `top_fixes`: up to five, most valuable first. Each names the route or section, the problem and the change to make.
